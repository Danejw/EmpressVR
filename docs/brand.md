# EmpressVR: brand identity and website design reference

> **Status:** Development reference extracted from the client-provided **Empress Immersive LTD Branding & Marketing Guide (2025)**, a **work-in-progress** document. It is a guide to the current brand, **not a final client-approved redesign specification**. Use the original PDF when checking the visual examples. This repository is public, so the full source PDF, unpublished campaign plans, and internal personal/contact information are intentionally not committed.

## 1. What EmpressVR is

**Business:** Empress Immersive LTD, trading publicly as **EmpressVR**.

**Core identity (source, p. 3):** A community-powered XR studio creating innovative immersive experiences while elevating the next generation of diverse creative technologists.

**Purpose (pp. 3–6):** Amplify diverse voices through immersive storytelling, expand access to XR, support creative empowerment, and nurture opportunities for artists, technologists, storytellers, researchers, and underrepresented creators.

**What it makes (pp. 4–6):** Virtual reality, spatial audio, interactive environments, immersive art and performance, and research-informed work touching culture and wellbeing. The studio pursues artistic, educational, collaborative, and commercial projects.

**Co-operative values (p. 3):** Care, democracy, equity, equality, honesty, openness, self-help, self-responsibility, solidarity, and social responsibility. The wider brand emphasizes ethical innovation, collective ownership, inclusive practice, transparency, and creativity.

**Brand character (pp. 3–4):** **Bold, curious, experimental, deeply human**. Warm, collaborative, welcoming, future-facing, at times creatively rebellious. Community-first and accessible, even when discussing cutting-edge technology.

**Positioning (pp. 3–4):** A cross-disciplinary creative studio where immersive art, community-led talent development, and digital therapeutic research intersect. Be careful about clinical claims: the existence of research-informed work does not authorize unverified claims of therapeutic effectiveness.

**Primary website audiences (pp. 5–6):** Artists and creative technologists, prospective community members and collaborators, learners, audiences seeking immersive experiences, and relevant arts/culture/wellbeing organizations. Validate individual calls to action against real active offerings before publishing.

## 2. Visual identity: fixed source specifications

### Official color palette (source, p. 7)

| Level | Name in guide | Hex | Design role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | White | `#FFFFFF` | Backgrounds, legible content |
| Primary | Whitish Blue | `#DADDEC` | Soft panels and lighter surface treatments |
| Primary | Dull Blue | `#171D3A` | Dark navy sections, core text, contrast |
| Secondary | Pale Magenta | `#ED68E7` | Energetic section treatments and highlights |
| Secondary | Pale Green | `#8DF382` | Energetic complementary blocks and emphasis |
| Secondary | Hot Orange | `#F36000` | Occasional bold highlights |
| Accent | Violet | `#7700FF` | Deliberate accent, emphasis or distinctive section |
| Accent | Lime Yellow | `#CCFF00` | Sparing call-to-action/accent |
| Accent | Dark Green | `#14CC00` | Sparing call-to-action/accent |

**Color hierarchy from the guide:** The three primary colors establish the base. Secondary colors are complementary; accents should be **used sparingly**. The guide's Squarespace examples (pp. 8–10) show assertive color-blocking and alternating light, navy, magenta, green and purple areas. Favor intentionally composed color sections over decorating every component with every color.

Do not invent additional official brand colors. Extra neutral shades may be used for usability only as **implementation tokens**, clearly separate from the official palette. Check accessible foreground/background contrast, especially when bright green or magenta is involved.

### Typography (source, p. 9)

| Context | Preferred font | Source note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website headings | **Young Serif** | Named website heading face |
| Website body | **Adelle Sans** | Guide lists **Fira Sans** as free alternative |
| Procreate headings | Optima Bold | Used in other brand artwork |
| Procreate body | Palatino | Used in other brand artwork |

Design with Young Serif headings and Adelle Sans body when the necessary font licensing/assets are available; Fira Sans is a supported alternative for the body. Avoid silently treating Georgia or generic system fonts in the current starter as final approved typography. The logo lettering is specially stretched Optima-based artwork, not ordinary text set in a web font.

### Logo and symbols (source, pp. 1, 9)

- **Preferred marks:** The original EmpressVR crown/wordmark and the **square or round cosmic logo**, which the guide explicitly calls a favorite.
- Existing variants: black on white, white on black, simple wordmark, and circular crown treatment.
- Preserve the **original crown geometry, wordmark proportions and cosmic artwork**. Use approved source assets; do not approximate, trace, or AI-recreate the logo as a production substitute.
- Give the mark clear space and strong contrast. Avoid squeezing the round lockup into narrow navigation areas; use an approved horizontal wordmark if available.

**Asset dependency:** Request approved original logo files (preferably SVG plus transparent PNG), licensed fonts and original project imagery. Until received, label any temporary marks as placeholders.

## 3. Visual taste and page composition

The following are **design interpretations** derived from the screenshots on pp. 8–10, combined with the explicit palette, typography and personality statements above. These are useful directions for the rebuild, not previously approved components.

**Look and feel:** Expressive, immersive, colorful and art-led, with a friendly human presence. A confident editorial serif can sit alongside playful saturated sections, cinematic artwork and generous breathing room. Technical subjects should still feel approachable.

**Patterns worth carrying forward:**

- **Split hero composition:** A strong headline and purpose-led copy paired with immersive illustration, a real artwork or VR media (p. 10).
- **Alternating themed sections:** Light / dark navy / pale green / magenta / occasional purple sections with clear text contrast (pp. 8–10).
- **Project showcases:** Real images from productions or installations, paired with project titles and direct links. Let the artwork carry visual impact (p. 10).
- **People and co-operative identity:** Visible team/member imagery and readable introductions, with a clear path to community participation (p. 10).
- **Distinct but restrained CTAs:** The screenshots show rounded-pill buttons in brand colors. Use consistent primary, secondary and quiet/link variations rather than many unrelated button styles (pp. 8–10).
- **Footer identity:** The guide shows the logo, company name, company number and contact information in the site footer (p. 9). Confirm current legal entity details before implementing.

**Taste guardrails:** Make creative decisions that are specific to EmpressVR's artwork and community. Avoid generic SaaS layouts, stock corporate images, decorative card grids with no content purpose, faux-futuristic clichés, inaccessible neon text, over-animated pages, or unlicensed VR imagery. Maintain coherent spacing and composition across pages even when each project has distinctive artwork.

**Accessibility and interaction:** The brand values care and accessibility (pp. 5–6). Make those values tangible through readable typography, inclusive image descriptions, clear booking/payment steps, keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, responsive layouts and unambiguous feedback.

## 4. Voice, content and user experience

**Writing voice:** Warm, curious, confident, collaborative, imaginative and plain enough for newcomers. Welcome learners and community participants without assuming everyone knows XR jargon. Use accurate descriptions of actual offerings and meaningful, specific calls to action.

**Words and spelling:** Use the brand names **EmpressVR** and **Empress Immersive LTD** consistently. The guide uses British English such as *centre*, *wellbeing*, and *co-operative*. Follow the client's current preferred spelling and approved copy.

**Information priorities (source, pp. 5–6, 10):**

1. Establish who the studio is and why it exists.
2. Show actual immersive projects and the people behind them.
3. Provide obvious ways to explore, learn, collaborate, join a community or attend a real event.
4. Make active bookings, purchasing and donating safe, accessible and trustworthy.

**Content accuracy:** Do not invent people, credentials, impact metrics, case studies, projects, sponsors, schedules or testimonials. Clearly mark placeholder content until verified against the Squarespace site/client materials. Preserve important existing content, legal pages and discoverability during migration.

**Not a requirement:** The latter half of the 2025 guide sketches possible marketing campaigns and channels, including unfinished SMART goals. Treat these as *draft context*, not approved current website functionality or live campaign commitments (pp. 11–15).

## 5. Development application and review criteria

**Before building any new page or shared component:**

- Read this file and the source PDF pages applicable to the feature. Use `.cursor/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md` for design and `.cursor/skills/web-design-guidelines/SKILL.md` for UI review.
- Establish the page's audience, real content, primary task, visual hierarchy and desired outcome.
- Reuse existing tokens, typography roles, nav, CTAs and module patterns. Favor composable sections and authentic EmpressVR media.
- For changes to visual identity, distinguish *source requirements* from a new design proposal. Obtain approval before treating new art direction as the brand's canonical look.

**Visual acceptance checklist:** Brand typography and palette; correct original logo/real imagery; clear hierarchy and visual rhythm; desktop/mobile quality; legible CTAs; keyboard/contrast/motion accessibility; no fake business content; consistent responsive components; screenshots checked against intended direction.

**Current starter gap:** `src/app/globals.css` currently uses fallback fonts, generic layout treatments and a text-symbol placeholder for the logo. Those are **implementation placeholders**. This guide documents the target direction but does not change the site UI in this commit.
